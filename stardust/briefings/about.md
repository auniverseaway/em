# Intent

Tell the reader who Chris is, why he writes, where the boundaries of the publication are. Build trust quickly. Doubles as a colophon — the production notes for the publication itself.

# Audience

Readers who finished an article and want to know "who is this guy" — typically engineers in the AEM/EDS world, peers at Adobe, occasionally journalists or recruiters. Almost always arrive from an end-of-article callout, not direct.

# Key Messages

1. Chris Millar works at Adobe. *Experience Managed* is independent of Adobe.
2. Pieces exist because there's something the writer wanted in writing. Critical-friend register, bounded by "things that won't get me fired."
3. Loyal to humans making things — AI-assisted creators included.

# Calls to Action

- Primary: Read the latest piece (single Underline-tertiary CTA, end of page)
- Secondary: RSS · social-lockup (chrome)
- Contact: email or social DM ([TBD] — designer to choose handle/address)

# Tone

Warmer than article pages; first-person ("I"). Honest about constraints (the Adobe boundary, the low cadence, the lack of editorial calendar). Short, declarative paragraphs. No bullet-list "About me" résumé. The page reads like a letter, not a profile.
