§ 02 · The palette

02

Five colors. Reading among trees. No cream, no pinks, no Adobe red.

Stone

#EEF0EC

Primary surface. Page ground. Pale cool lichen with a hair of green undertone — verified non-cream by warm-bias test.

Loam

#E2E6DF

Secondary surface. Code-block ground. Sidebar and tonal-block fill within long reads.

Canopy

#008000

Brand anchor. Links, wordmark disc, eyebrow accents, code-block left rule, hover under-line. CSS named green.

Pine

#0A4F1C

Hover state for Canopy. Code keywords. Eyebrow color (Pine reads quieter than Canopy at 11px).

Trunk

#1A1A1A

Body text, headings, CTA fill. Near-black with a hair of warmth — sits with Stone rather than pure ink.

Why these. CSS named green as anchor is a knowing position — most "design-aware" brands flee primary CSS keywords in 2026. Owning it is a stance. Stone and Loam reject the assistant's strongest default (cream/vellum/oatmeal) — see _divergence.off_toolbox_moves in the brand profile.

  const candidates = asset.renditions
    .filter((r) => r.format === 'webp')
    .sort((a, b) => a.width - b.width);

    ?? candidates.at(-1)

§ 04 · Voice

04

A working CMS practitioner who writes when there's something they actually want in writing.

This is

  • Authoritative-yet-curious
  • Plainspoken
  • Opinionated
  • Crafted

This is not

  • Vendor-neutral / stenographic
  • Sycophantic to the platform
  • Anti-AI / gatekeeping toward AI-assisted creators
  • Clickbait / listicle-bait
  • Adobe-corporate-comms register
★ Do

"The seven deadly sins of Edge Delivery development."

Punchy, opinionated. List-as-position, not list-as-bait. Names the platform plainly.

★ Do

"The tension between brand equity and making money."

Holds a contradiction without resolving it. Trusts the reader to do the work.

★ Do

"Claude can be shockingly good at polish if you let it."

Surprised observer. "If you let it" credits human and tool together — the brand's loyalty position in one sentence.

⌀ Don't

"Unleash the power of Edge Delivery to supercharge your content velocity!"

Marketing-collateral voice. "Unleash", "supercharge", exclamation point, abstract benefit-promise.

⌀ Don't

"In this comprehensive guide, we'll walk through everything you need to know about AEM."

Generic guide-blog opening. No position. Reader has no reason to keep reading.

⌀ Don't

"AI is killing creativity. Real designers don't use Claude."

Anti-AI gatekeeping. Violates the brand's loyalty to humans making things — including those using AI.

Hard rules

  1. Pieces exist because there's something the writer wants in writing. Never to fill a queue.
  2. Take a position in the lede or kill the piece.
  3. Never write 'leverage'. Never write 'unleash'. Never write 'comprehensive guide'.
  4. No exclamation points except in dialogue or when reporting someone else's exclaim.
  5. Em-dashes welcome; semicolons earned.
  6. AI-assisted craft is craft. Name the tool specifically (Claude, Codex) when it's load-bearing.
  7. Critical of Adobe: fine. Hostile to Adobe: not. The boundary is "things that won't get me fired."

§ 05 · Tone & writing goals

05

Tone scales by pillar. Opinion runs longer. Field notes are tight. Code patterns serve the technique.

1

To Position

Opinion pieces stake a claim. The first paragraph names the position; the rest earns it. 1200–2400 words.

2

To Equip

Code patterns hand the reader a working technique. Code is the spine; prose connects the moves. 600–1200 words.

3

To Witness

Field notes record what the writer saw — a specific project, a specific bug, a specific surprise. No conclusion required. 300–800 words.

Be clever

The opening line of an Opinion piece. The closing sentence of a Field note. One section title per article, maximum.

Be clear

Default register. Always for code patterns. Always for any sentence inside a long-form that introduces a new term.

§ 06 · Motifs

06

Five gestures. Used sparingly — earn their presence by being rare.

Managed

Period disc

The trailing period of the wordmark, rendered as a small Canopy disc. ~14% of cap height. At small sizes the disc stands alone as the brand's smallest mark.

→ Always green. Section headlines may end with one disc — at most once per page.

Atmospheric photography ground

Big abstract forest imagery sits behind the article hero — canopy, leaf-shadow, bark, river-surface, fog. Out-of-focus / motion-blurred / macro. Never sharp documentary.

→ Every article carries one. Photography is the production value.

Fine Canopy rule

1px Canopy line as section divider. Replaces the typical heavy editorial rule. Very thin, very specific.

→ Major section breaks within an article. Never as ornament, never doubled.

07

Editorial numeral

Section numbers set in IvyOra Display 100wt (Thin), very large (96–120px), Canopy color, alongside the section title.

→ Long-form Opinion only. Skip on Field notes and Code patterns.

Subject DNA

  • Canopy, leaf-shadow, bark, foliage
  • River surface, fog through trees
  • Stone, lichen, moss, rain on leaves
  • Out-of-focus / motion-blurred / macro
  • Color cast: green / earth / shadow — cool, never warm
  • Hero runs full-bleed; type sits over it with a Stone or Trunk overlay

Never

  • Stock business imagery
  • Isometric SaaS illustrations
  • AI-generated faces (or AI photos that read as AI)
  • Screenshots-as-hero — screenshots are figures, not heroes
  • Warm-orange / sunset / golden-hour grade
  • People-of-the-platform smiling at the camera

§ 09 · Content pillars

09

Three pillars. Low cadence. High signal.

01

Opinion

Long-form positions on industry direction, platform decisions, the state of CMS work. The thought-leadership read for C-suite; the position-piece for engineers who want a take.

02

Code patterns

Short, reusable techniques for AEM and Edge Delivery — copy-paste-friendly, no framework required. Excellent code typography; layout gets out of the way.

03

Field notes

Short observations from real client work. A logbook register: specific, dated, lower stakes. The pillar that builds credibility through accumulation.

§ 10 · Logo & wordmark

10

"Experience Managed" set in IvyOra Display. The period is a Canopy disc.

Clear space

Maintain clear space equal to the height of the trailing period-disc on all sides of the wordmark.

Don't

  • Don't recolor the disc to anything other than Canopy.
  • Don't bold the wordmark above 400 weight — the lightness is the point.
  • Don't set the wordmark in any face other than IvyOra Display.
  • Don't add a tagline or descriptor inside the lockup.
