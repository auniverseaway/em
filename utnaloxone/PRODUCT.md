# Utah Naloxone — PRODUCT

register: brand

## Product purpose

Utah Naloxone is a public-health initiative that puts free naloxone (Narcan) into the
hands of everyday Utahns so opioid overdoses can be reversed before they become deaths.
The site exists to *communicate and mobilize*, not to transact: get a kit, get trained,
learn the signs, find a location, donate, share a story.

## Users

- People who use drugs, and the people who love them.
- Bystanders and community members who could be first on the scene.
- Healthcare workers, librarians, fire stations, recovery advocates who distribute kits.
- Donors and partners funding the work.

Mood at the moment of visit: often scared, often grieving, sometimes ashamed. The site
must lower the temperature, remove stigma, and make the next action feel possible.

## Brand voice (three physical words)

**Warm. Hopeful. Hand-on-your-shoulder.** Not clinical, not fear-mongering, not preachy.
Naloxone is reframed from emergency equipment into an everyday act of care.

Positioning line: **"Every day is a second chance."**

## Tone rules

- Talk to a person, not a patient. Plain, kind, direct.
- Name the stakes honestly (10 Utahns die each week) but never weaponize fear or shame.
- Dignity first: "people who use drugs," never "addicts." Recovery is possible; relapse is not failure.

## Anti-references (what to avoid)

- Sterile hospital white-and-teal. The category-reflex healthcare look.
- Stock "sad person by a window" pity imagery.
- Government-PSA stiffness; dense clinical brochures.
- Fear-based "this could be you" shock tactics.

## Strategic principles

- The single most important action is **getting naloxone**. Everything orients to that.
- Hope is the differentiator. Light canvas, sunrise palette, second-chance narrative.
- Distinctiveness over safety: harm-reduction deserves a brand that feels alive.
